## Hidden problems (checklist)
1. **Unsorted `order`** in requirements.json.
2. **Identical-content duplicate** with different names: `experience_certificate.pdf` / `scan_0042.pdf`.
3. **Expired**: bank solvency (2026-09-30 < 2026-10-20).
4. **Expires exactly on deadline**: tax clearance 2026-10-20 — boundary case; rule = valid if `expiry >= deadline`.
5. **Fake .pdf**: `bid_security_BG.pdf` is a PNG — extension/MIME lie; detect via `%PDF-` header.
6. **Corrupt PDF**: `power_of_attorney.pdf` has a valid header but broken body — header check alone is not enough; must try parsing.
7. **Password-protected PDF**: `ISO_9001_certificate.pdf`.
8. **Missing mandatory**: manufacturer authorization (no file at all); bid security and power of attorney become missing too because their files are unusable.
9. **Irrelevant extra file**: company brochure.
10. **Misleading / opaque names**: `tax_certificate.pdf` is VAT/BIN (not tax clearance); `IT-2026.pdf` is tax clearance; `scan_0042.pdf` gives no hint.
11. **Date formats vary**: `30 June 2027`, `20/10/2026` (DD/MM/YYYY — not US), `2026-09-30`. Expiry is entered by the user, not auto-trusted.

## Generalised traps to expect in the unseen pack
- Duplicates by hash regardless of name; a duplicate may be matched to two requirements — warn.
- Multi-page PDFs (page counts must be summed in the package/footer numbering).
- Header `%PDF-` may appear after leading junk bytes (spec allows within first 1024 B) → search, don't require offset 0.
- Optional docs missing/broken must not block generation; mandatory ones must.
- Deadline may already have passed relative to today → show "deadline passed".
- requirements.json may use alternate keys (`deadline` vs `submission_deadline`, `required` vs `mandatory`) → parser accepts aliases and reports clear errors.
